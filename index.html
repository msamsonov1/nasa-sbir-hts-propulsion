Executive Driving Mechanism

The core driving mechanism is a cryogen-free, conduction-cooled rare-earth barium copper oxide high-temperature superconducting coil assembly operating between $20\text{ K}$ and $50\text{ K}$ via solid-state pulse-tube cryocoolers and thermal busbars, engineered with a non-insulated winding topology that passively bypasses quench hot-spots through turn-to-turn radial current sharing without the mass penalty of active detection electronics or dump resistors.

1. Identification and Significance of the Innovation

Deep-space transit architectures—including crewed Mars missions, outer-planet probes, and high-energy orbital transfer vehicles—are strictly limited by the thrust-to-weight and specific impulse trade-offs of chemical and fission-electric systems. Fusion-enabled propulsion architectures such as the Direct Fusion Drive, Field-Reversed Configurations, and Magneto-Inertial Propulsion offer theoretical specific power ratings exceeding $P_{\text{specific}} > 1\text{ kW/kg}$ with specific impulses ranging from $I_{sp} \approx 5{,}000\text{ s}$ to $15{,}000\text{ s}$. Realizing these capabilities requires on-axis magnetic field strengths between $B = 8\text{ T}$ and $15\text{ T}$ to compress plasma or collimate high-enthalpy exhaust streams through magnetic nozzles.

Conventional Low-Temperature Superconductors (NbTi, $\text{Nb}_3\text{Sn}$) are practically non-viable for flight architectures because they require liquid helium cooling at $T \approx 4.2\text{ K}$. Maintaining liquid helium introduces severe launch-mass penalties, cryogen boil-off risks, and massive radiator area requirements. Under the Stefan-Boltzmann radiation law:

$$\dot{Q}_{\text{rad}} = \epsilon \sigma A \left( T_{\text{rad}}^4 - T_{\text{sink}}^4 \right)$$

Operating at elevated cryogenic temperatures ($20\text{ K to } 50\text{ K}$) cuts radiator surface area and mass by over $80\%$. RF WaveCode has structured this proposal around three interlocking hardware innovations designed for laboratory co-development:

  • Cryogen-Free Conduction Cooling: Flexible aluminum and pyrolytic graphite busbars coupling coil windings directly to closed-cycle cryocoolers.
  • Non-Insulated (NI) Windings: Passive self-protecting turn-to-turn current sharing preventing burnout without massive quench dump resistors.
  • Composite Mandrels: Additively manufactured CTE-matched titanium/carbon fiber mandrels withstanding hoop stresses up to $600\text{ MPa}$ with engineered current density $J_e \ge 400\text{ A/mm}^2$.

2. Phase I Technical Objectives & Risk Mitigations

Objective 1: Coupled Electromechanical and Screening-Current Stress FEA

Led by RF WaveCode software simulation tools. Quantifies internal shear and radial peel stresses to ensure strain stays well below $\epsilon < 0.4\%$ under dynamic charging.

Objective 2: Cryothermal Busbar Optimization & Fast-Neutron Attenuation

Multi-physics conduction model maintaining a winding-to-sink gradient $\Delta T < 3\text{ K}$ under secondary plasma radiation loads ($50\text{ W/m}^2$).

Objective 3: Dynamic Charging Delay and Passive Quench Lab Testing

Winding and vacuum cryogenic evaluation of a 50–100 mm sub-scale test coil down to 30 K to empirically calibrate charging delays ($\tau = L_{\text{coil}} / R_{\text{contact}}$).

Objective 4: Phase II Scale-Up Blueprint & Flight Roadmap

CAD tooling designs, manufacturing bills of material, and institutional test protocols for full-scale $\ge 10\text{ T}$ prototype coil fabrication.
